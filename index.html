From a distance the paintings read like photographs. Step closer and the brushwork appears: layered glazes, the weave of the canvas, the slow build of a single petal.

The artist

Painting what you walk past

Osmolovska paints flowers and fruit at a scale that makes you stop. A lotus becomes a metre of folded silk; a grape becomes a small planet beaded with water.

Each painting starts from her own photographs and takes weeks of slow, layered work in oil. The dark grounds of the Night Garden series are built so the colour of every petal seems lit from within.

“I want people to see the flower the way a bee does, close enough to get lost in it.”
